DEMO BANK UZ
CUSTOMER RECORDS AND INTERNAL CONTROL POLICY

SYNTHETIC TEST DOCUMENT — NOT FOR OPERATIONAL USE

Document version: 2.0 (synthetic assessment-test revision)
Revision date: 11 October 2026
Organization: Demo Bank UZ, a fictional commercial bank
Responsible departments: Compliance and Customer Operations

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This synthetic policy describes customer due diligence, payment services,
anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist/proliferation-financing controls,
personal-data handling, record retention, and related internal governance at
fictional Demo Bank UZ. It applies to the Bank's branches, digital channels,
employees, contractors, and controlled service processes. It covers retail and
legal-entity customers where the Bank provides the relevant service.

For this test document, assume Demo Bank UZ is a commercial bank that provides
bank-account and payment services, including payment orders and payment cards,
and processes personal data. The Bank is not assumed to be a payment-system
operator, a separately licensed payment organization, an electronic-money
issuer, or an operator of a significant payment system. Those activities and
their additional duties apply only after Compliance verifies formal designation,
authorization, and actual operations. Payment-agent requirements apply only
while the Bank has an applicable agency/subagency arrangement. Retail-merchant
card-acceptance duties do not apply merely because the Bank serves retail
customers. Compliance maintains an activity/applicability register and records
the evidence supporting each role decision; uncertainty is referred for legal
review, not treated as an exemption.

This file is a synthetic demonstration artifact, not an approved or operational
bank policy. The cited legal corpus and AI assessment are not a substitute for
checking current official texts, implementing rules, effective dates, the
Bank's actual license and activities, and advice from qualified Uzbekistan
legal/compliance professionals. If this document conflicts with applicable
law, the law controls and Compliance must escalate and document remediation.

2. CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION

Before activating an account, Customer Operations must obtain the customer's
full name, date of birth, residential address, and an approved identity-document
number. Staff must compare the customer with the identity document and record
the method and date of verification.

For digital onboarding, the customer must complete a live video check. A copy
of the identity document is retained in the customer file. If the image is
unclear, the account must not be activated until a staff member has reviewed
the document.

3. CUSTOMER RECORDS

Customer Operations stores customer-identification records in the bank's
restricted customer-document system. Access is limited to staff whose assigned
work requires the records. Staff must not download customer files to personal
devices or send them through personal email.

The system records the staff account and time when a customer file is opened.
The Compliance team reviews the access log once each calendar year. A manager
may request an additional review after a suspected unauthorized access event.

The Bank retains transaction information, payer/beneficiary identification
data, customer-identification and due-diligence materials, and related control
records for at least five years after the relevant payment service/operation
or termination of the business relationship, using the later applicable trigger
where records fall under more than one requirement. A longer retention period
required by applicable law, a lawful hold, an investigation, or an authorized
instruction takes precedence. Compliance owns the retention schedule and
documents the record category, trigger, and any longer period. Records must
not be destroyed while a hold or investigation is active. At expiry, the
records owner verifies that no hold or other retention duty applies, obtains
documented approval, securely destroys or irreversibly anonymizes the records,
and records the disposition. The former three-year account-closure rule is
withdrawn and must not be used for records covered by this clause.

4. CUSTOMER RISK REVIEW

At onboarding, the Customer Operations officer assigns a low, medium, or high
customer-risk category using the customer's country of residence and expected
account activity. A high-risk classification requires approval from a
Compliance manager before the account is activated.

Compliance assigns each customer a documented risk level using customer,
beneficial-owner, geography, product, channel, and expected-activity factors.
The Bank verifies the identity and authority of customers and representatives,
examines legal-entity ownership and control, identifies beneficial owners, and
keeps customer and owner information current. Ongoing monitoring compares
transactions with the customer's known profile and expected activity. Reviews
take place at onboarding, on material changes or alerts, and at a risk-based
periodic interval recorded in the AML procedures. Higher-risk cases receive
enhanced review and approval by the designated Compliance officer before the
relevant relationship or activity proceeds, where required.

Compliance documents at least annually the Bank's money-laundering, terrorism-
financing, and proliferation-financing risk assessment, resulting controls,
owners, and remediation actions. Control intensity and resources must reflect
the recorded risk. Material results and relevant changes are communicated to
responsible business units and governing functions.

Before onboarding and as required by the current AML procedures, the Bank
screens customers, beneficial owners, representatives, and transaction parties
against applicable terrorism and proliferation lists and other legally required
lists. A potential match is escalated promptly to Compliance for resolution;
staff must not override a match or tip off a customer. Where a legally covered
person or property is identified, the Bank follows the applicable immediate
restriction, suspension/freezing, permitted-crediting exception, and reporting
requirements without prior notice where required by law. A legal hold or
authorized instruction controls over ordinary processing.

If required customer verification cannot be completed, staff must not open the
account, start or continue the covered business relationship, or execute the
covered operation; the case is escalated to Compliance to determine termination
and reporting duties under the applicable rule. Operations must be refused when
required identification documents are not provided, subject only to a verified
legal exception (including any applicable incoming-credit exception).

Employees must escalate suspicious attempted, ongoing, or completed activity
to Compliance immediately through the restricted reporting channel. Compliance
documents its decision and submits reports to the specially authorized state
body within the applicable legal deadline; the current corpus identifies a
maximum of one working day after detection for covered suspicious transactions.
Compliance also assesses reporting duties for covered non-cooperating-state
parties and binding decisions of the specially authorized state body. Staff
must not disclose that a control measure, screening, or report is underway to
the customer or an unauthorized person. AML information access is restricted
to authorized personnel and is not disseminated except as legally permitted.

5. RECORD REQUESTS

Requests from courts, the Central Bank, the specially authorized state body,
and other public authorities are sent immediately to Legal and Compliance for
authentication, scope, deadline, confidentiality, and lawful-disclosure review.
The accountable owner records the request, legal basis, materials supplied,
approvals, and response date; preserves relevant records; and meets the verified
statutory or instruction deadline. No employee may disclose banking-secret,
AML-control, or personal data outside the Bank without documented authorization
and a lawful basis. This review must not delay an immediate mandatory freeze,
suspension, or report. Staff must preserve the confidentiality of AML measures
and prohibit tipping off.

6. RESPONSIBILITIES AND EXCEPTIONS

The Head of Customer Operations is responsible for operational implementation.
The Chief Compliance Officer owns AML controls, suspicious-activity escalation,
legal reporting, role/applicability decisions, and monitoring of remediation.
The Data Protection Officer or designated privacy owner oversees personal-data
controls. Information Security owns technical safeguards and incident
coordination. Internal Audit independently tests control design and operation.
All relevant staff receive role-based induction and recurring training;
Compliance records completion and follows up overdue training.

Exceptions are prohibited where they would bypass a legal requirement, customer
verification, sanctions/list screening, a required payment authorization, a
retention hold, or a mandatory reporting/freezing duty. A permitted operational
exception requires a documented reason, risk assessment, compensating controls,
named approver, expiry date, and Compliance review. The digital video-check
method may be replaced only by another currently approved, sufficiently
reliable identity-verification method; inability to complete verification
does not itself authorize account activation.

7. POLICY REVIEW

Compliance reviews this policy at least annually and after a relevant legal,
regulatory, product, channel, license, outsourcing, incident, or risk change.
Compliance monitors official regulatory sources, records relevant changes,
assigns an accountable owner and due date, assesses affected controls and
policies, and documents approval and implementation. Legal or Compliance
verifies the current official text, effective date, applicability, and any
implementing rules before treating an AI-generated alert or assessment as a
requirement. The Bank tracks Central Bank instructions applicable to the Bank,
assigns remediation owners and deadlines, and retains evidence of completion.

8. PAYMENT SERVICE CONTROLS

The following controls apply when the Bank performs the relevant payment
service or role. The payment operations owner maintains procedures and system
controls for the Bank's actual payer-bank, beneficiary-bank, card, transfer,
and agent roles. A role-specific legal applicability decision is recorded
before a duty is treated as applicable or excluded.

8.1 Authorization, execution, and records. A payment service is provided only
on the user's valid payment order and under its recorded conditions and
particulars. Staff and systems must not alter an order's terms or initiate a
payment without the user's order, except where a verified law expressly permits
or requires otherwise. Payment documents contain required payer and beneficiary
details, subject to verified statutory exceptions. The value date must not be
more than ten days after the issue date. Controls validate the order, authority,
available information, value date, and applicable execution deadline before
release; rejected orders are handled and explained under the applicable
procedure. The Bank gives the user a paper or electronic confirmation of the
payment service with required particulars.

8.2 Timing, finality, errors, and charges. Payment operations monitor receipt,
execution, settlement, and beneficiary-account credit timestamps. A payment is
treated as final at the legally applicable event; for a payment-system
participant this includes credit to the beneficiary participant's account.
Where a value date falls on a non-working day, the beneficiary-bank process
credits the beneficiary on the first following working day, where that role
applies. An erroneous payment attributable to the Bank is investigated without
delay and corrected by transferring funds to the recipient no later than the
next working day after detection when the cited rule applies. Delays, refunds,
claims, and statutory penalties are escalated to Legal/Compliance and calculated
under the verified current rule; the relevant payment-provider provision in
the selected corpus states 0.1 percent per day subject to a 10 percent cap.
Users are informed of the applicable commission before service. A commission
may not be deducted from the payment amount unless the required contract
permits it; the beneficiary receives information on amounts received and
deductions upon request. Tariffs and customer notices are approved, accurate,
version-controlled, and published where the Bank's role requires publication.

8.3 Agents, complaints, and continuity. Before any payment-agent or subagent
is engaged, Legal/Compliance confirms the Bank's role and required agreement,
registration, user disclosures, remuneration, evidence/receipt, reporting,
and oversight controls. The Bank maintains the applicable agent/subagent
register, checks contractual compliance, and promptly escalates incidents or
missing agent information. The Bank remains accountable for obligations that
law assigns jointly with its agents. Payment-service complaints and system
incidents are logged, assigned, investigated, answered within verified
deadlines, and analyzed for recurring consumer harm. Information security,
cybersecurity, access control, recovery, and continuity measures protect the
Bank's payment services. Central Bank remediation instructions are tracked to
completion within their applicable deadlines. Requirements specific to a
payment-system operator, a payment organization, a significant system, or a
separately licensed service are not assumed merely from the Bank's status; if
the role is confirmed, Compliance adds the corresponding approved controls.

9. AML/CFT/PROLIFERATION-FINANCING REPORTING AND CONTROL

The AML control program includes written risk-based procedures, identity and
beneficial-owner checks, ongoing monitoring, list screening, escalation,
reporting, recordkeeping, confidentiality, and trained accountable personnel.
Controls cover operations prepared, attempted, ongoing, and completed where
the relevant rule applies. Compliance keeps an auditable case record of alerts,
analysis, decisions, reports, instructions, restrictions, and approvals, with
access limited to authorized staff. Reports and suspicions are not disclosed
to customers. Procedures reflect the current detailed AML rules and authorized
state-body decisions; this summary does not replace them.

10. PERSONAL DATA AND INFORMATION SECURITY

The Bank processes personal data for specified, communicated purposes and
records the applicable lawful basis for each process. Privacy notices explain
processing purposes and subject rights when data is entered into a database,
where required. Consent is obtained in a form that proves receipt when consent
is the required basis. Written consent, including electronic-document form,
is obtained for special personal data where required; the privacy owner
verifies representative/parent/guardian/heir rules before processing such
cases. A consent withdrawal is recorded and handled in the received form or
another legally accepted written/electronic form; processing then stops or
continues only on a verified alternative legal basis.

Access to personal data is role-based and limited to professional duties.
Employees and processors must preserve confidentiality and may not disclose
or distribute personal data without consent or another verified legal basis.
Contracts and due diligence govern third-party processors, confidentiality,
security, permitted purposes, return/deletion, and incident notification. A
third-party transfer is documented, reviewed for legal basis and safeguards,
and notified to the subject in writing within the applicable period; the
selected legal text includes a three-day notice requirement for covered
transfers. Automated decisions receive a process explanation, consequences,
and objection channel where required; objections are investigated and answered
in writing within the applicable deadline, including ten days where the cited
rule applies.

The privacy owner maintains an inventory of personal-data databases and
determines which must be registered, which exception (if any) is supported,
and whether registration details require an update. Changes to registration
information are notified to the authorized body within the applicable period;
the selected text includes ten calendar days. Required personal data is stored
within Uzbekistan where the law applies, including the stated categories of
biometric, genetic, and telecommunications-user data. Legal, organizational,
and technical safeguards protect confidentiality, integrity, and availability
from collection through secure destruction or anonymization. Suspected access,
loss, alteration, or disclosure is reported immediately to Security and
Compliance, contained, investigated, documented, and reported to authorities
and affected persons when current law requires.

Data is corrected or supplemented on a subject request within the applicable
deadline; inaccuracies discovered by the Bank are corrected immediately where
required. Data is destroyed or anonymized when its documented purpose is
achieved, a controlling consent is withdrawn, an applicable processing period
expires, or a court decision requires it, unless a lawful retention duty or
hold requires continued retention. The privacy owner records the legal basis
for any continued retention, access restriction, change, or destruction and
provides required written notices.

11. PAYMENT-SERVICE RETENTION AND PRIVACY RETENTION

The five-year minimum in section 3 is a floor for covered payment, AML,
identification, and due-diligence records, not permission to retain personal
data without purpose. The records owner maps each category to both its legal
minimum and privacy purpose, applies any longer statutory period or lawful
hold, then securely destroys or anonymizes data when no retention basis
remains. Conflicts between retention and deletion duties are escalated to
Legal/Compliance and documented; staff must not resolve them by silently
shortening a mandatory minimum or retaining data indefinitely.

12. CENTRAL BANK AND OTHER REGULATORY INTERACTIONS

Central Bank rulemaking, Board governance, appointments, monetary policy,
reserve-setting, licensing decisions, and supervisory powers belong to the
Central Bank and are not represented as duties of this Bank. The Bank does
cooperate with lawful supervisory requests applicable to it, protects
confidential information, validates and logs requests, submits accurate
required reports, and tracks binding instructions and remediation. Regulatory
filings, licensing, ownership, agent, incident, and organizational-change
notifications are made only when the Bank's actual role triggers them and
within the deadline confirmed from the current official rule.

13. CONTROL EVIDENCE AND TESTING

Each control owner retains dated evidence of execution, exceptions, review,
training, screening, reports, notices, payment timestamps, data-subject
requests, agent oversight, reconciliations, and remediation. Compliance
performs risk-based testing at least annually, records samples and results,
assigns corrective actions, and verifies closure. Internal Audit independently
tests material controls. Missing policy text or an automated assessment result
is not itself proof that an operational control failed; reviewers inspect
actual procedures and evidence before concluding.
